Decided: Atkinson Hyperlegible Next WhyIt was made for the hardest readers, people with low vision, so it is clear for everyone else too. It has character, so it doesn't look like every other app. It is free, has 7 weights and covers more than 150 languages. Runner-upB612, built by Airbus for glancing at cockpit screens. Dropped because it has only 2 weights and is wide, so headlines wrap sooner. EvidenceMIT AgeLab found that typefaces with open, rounded shapes (humanist) are read faster at a glance than tight, squared ones. Atkinson has open shapes. The other claims above come from each typeface's makers. Watch outNone of these cover Arabic, Bengali, Urdu or Chinese. Those fall back to the phone's own fonts, so we test them early.

Decide: how big is the opening line? Proposed44 pt Display. It holds about 15 characters per line on a small phone, which fits "Power is off." or "No phone signal." Longer headlines drop to Title 1. RulesText is left-aligned, never centred or justified. Numbers use tabular figures so times line up. Tracking stays near zero: Atkinson is already spaced for reading, so only the Display size is tightened, by 1%. Nothing goes below 13 pt, and only sources, times and labels use 13.

Decide: the state colours, and whether to offer a light mode ProposedDark by default. Brighter red and green for text on black. The SOS button keeps the deeper red, so white text on it passes. Why darkOn OLED phones a black pixel is off. Purdue measured 3–9% battery saved at 30–50% brightness and 39–47% at full brightness, which is where phones go outdoors. LCD phones save almost nothing. RulesRed only when a life may be at risk. Yellow is for icons, never text. No colour ever appears without its icon and word (section 6). OpenDark text on white reads better in bright sun and for some people with astigmatism. Do we follow the phone's light or dark setting, with the light palette above as the alternative?

5. Corners and depth

Flat surfaces with 1 px outlines, like a clean developer tool, but with softer corners so it feels friendly, not sharp. Four small radii, no pills. No shadows: they vanish on black.

4, tailThe New tag, and the corner of a message bubble nearest its sender
8, smallThings inside cards: the send button, story steps, the place box
12, cardCards, buttons, topic tiles, the text field, message bubbles
16, sheetAnything that slides up, and large panels

When one rounded shape sits inside another, the inner radius is the outer radius minus the gap. A 12 tile with 4 of padding holds an 8 box. Corners then look parallel instead of pinched.

Decide: how round? ProposedThe four radii above. Round enough to feel friendly, small enough to stay calm and precise. Pills are gone: a rounded rectangle reads as a button, a pill reads as decoration.

Motion

Movement only answers something the user did, or shows that something changed. Nothing pulses, glows or loops. Hover or focus a card to see the timing.

Press, 150 msA button or chip responds to a tap.
Expand, 250 msA card opens, a line fills, a delivery bar fills.
Sheet, 320 msA topic slides up over the home screen.

One easing curve for everything: fast start, gentle stop. With "Reduce motion" on, changes happen instantly.

Ten rules

The short version, for anyone designing a screen from this system. The reasoning is in PRINCIPLES.md.

  1. One typeface, two weights. Regular and Semibold.
  2. Eight text styles. Nothing in between.
  3. Left-aligned. Never centred, never justified.
  4. Black by default. Colour means state; hierarchy comes from size and weight.
  5. Never colour alone. Always an icon and a word.
  6. Yellow is never text. Icons on black, or a fill with black on it.
  7. Red is for lives. Not for errors or decoration.
  8. 4 px steps, 20 px margins. Space before lines and boxes.
  9. Small, friendly corners. 4, 8, 12, 16. No pills, no shadows.
  10. 44 pt to tap. Main actions are 48 pt high.
